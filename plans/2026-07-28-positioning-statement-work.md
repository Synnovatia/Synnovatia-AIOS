## Current Best Draft (tight positioning statement) — CONFIRMED 2026-07-29

> If you're a B2B service business owner generating $250K–$4M in revenue whose growth has stalled, I bring the kind of strategic thinking usually reserved for Fortune 500 companies, sized to get your business growing again. That comes from 25+ years running my own company and working alongside owners across a wide range of industries, which gives me both the big-picture view and the depth to understand your specific situation.

**POV decision (2026-07-29):** First person ("I"), not third person ("Jackie"/"Synnovatia"). Matches how Jackie spoke in Claude Projects previously, and preserves the "human peer, not detached expert" signal that was the strongest corroborated theme in the research (Mark Chapman, Christina Carlson, Anne Laguzza). A third-person "Synnovatia" version was drafted for comparison and rejected for the tight statement on those grounds — see Session 2 log below for the exact text if it's needed again for brand/institutional copy elsewhere.

### Session 2 edit trail (2026-07-29 — picks up after the 07-28 pause, logged so the next session doesn't repeat dead ends)

- Confirmed ending "not a template she's applying to it" from the 07-28 draft, then immediately revisited it under first-person POV (see below) — so it was never used in this exact third-person form in final copy.
- "30,000-ft view" as the core differentiator — **rejected as too thin.** It's literally just Raffi Saroyan's own metaphor for the Objectivity Gap theme (only Mark + Raffi, not the majority-signal "Dominant" tier), and "gives you perspective" is generic coach language every competitor can claim. Went back to the full `DiB_Interview_Synthesis_Master.docx` research and surfaced sharper, more specific alternatives: Two Funnels of Knowledge (Naya Troup — broad cross-industry pattern recognition AND deep specific-industry awareness, "most advisors have one, Jackie has both"), Articulating the Gut (Raffi), No Program/No System (Mark Chapman), Fear Removal (Mark Chapman).
- Jackie selected a cluster to build around: longevity + breadth as the source of the advice's weight, "two funnels" (broad + specific), no program/template, personalized/individualized attention, collaboration for achievable/practical solutions, and the Fortune 500-caliber-strategy-for-a-smaller-business scarcity angle (echoes Raffi's "no one else doing what Jackie does, at this level").
- First synthesis attempt tried to cram nearly all of that into one sentence — **too dense, read as a list.** Deliberately deferred "no program/methodology," "personalized/individualized attention," and "collaboration for achievable, practical solutions" to the fuller positioning brief (Still To Do #2) rather than force them into the tight statement.
- "giving me a read that's broad enough... grounded enough..." — **rejected**, "a read" isn't how Jackie talks, and "grounded enough" is too abstract to mean anything concrete to a client.
- "close enough to your day-to-day to know what's actually going on" — **rejected, important catch:** this phrasing accidentally echoes the *client's own* blind spot (the Objectivity Gap theme — "I was too close to see my own situation objectively," Mark Chapman) instead of contrasting with it. Using "close" language for Jackie's own vantage point undermines the outside-objectivity claim. Replaced with "the depth to understand your specific situation" instead.
- Two duplicate/style passes: eliminated a duplicated "B2B service business" (was used twice — opening clause and again re: "wide range of industries"), and removed em-dash-heavy parenthetical structure workspace-wide across this statement — read as choppy/AI-cadence rather than natural speech.
- Explicit AI-cadence scrub pass done at Jackie's request: removed "pattern recognition" (ML/AI-flavored term, not how a person describes their own judgment), collapsed a punchy negation-pivot ending ("X, not Y" tacked onto the close — a known AI-tell rhythm), fixed a person-consistency slip (opened in third-person "owners" but closed in second-person "yours").
- "not handed to you as a program or a template" — **rejected**, "handed to you" read awkward; simplified to "instead of delivered as a program or template" — then **later dropped entirely** once the "wall"/plateau framing (below) did equivalent "not generic, not one-size-fits-all" work on its own.
- New strategic input from Jackie (not previously documented anywhere in the workspace): the Messy Middle isn't one smooth stalled-growth stage — businesses plateau repeatedly at specific revenue thresholds ($250K–$400K, $750K–$1M, $3–4M) because they've outgrown their current strategy each time. This is sharper and more diagnosable than the generic "past scrappy, not self-running" stage descriptor that had been in the statement. **Important refinement, twice:** (1) the "outgrown their strategy" diagnosis is Jackie's insight, not something the business owner would say about their own problem — client-facing language needed to stay in the owner's actual self-perception ("growth stalled"), not the diagnosis. (2) same logic applied a second time — "whose growth **keeps** stalling" was rejected because "keeps" implies the owner has connected multiple stalls into a recognized pattern, which is again Jackie's pattern-recognition, not their lived experience. Each stall feels like an isolated, first-time event to the owner. Settled on "whose growth has stalled" — present-tense, singular, matches how an owner would actually describe their own situation.
- The three-plateau framework, and the "outgrown their strategy" diagnostic insight, are **reserved for the fuller positioning brief and website copy** — not the tight statement. They're strong problem-first hook material (per Craig Ullom's "lead with the customer's problem" principle) but too much numeric detail for a one-breath statement.
- "sized to get your business through the wall" — considered, then **rejected** in favor of "sized to get your business growing again," since "the wall" language was cut from the client-facing audience clause (see above) and "growing again" mirrors "stalled" directly without introducing wall/plateau imagery the tight statement no longer earns.
- "to get your business to the next level" — **rejected**, generic motivational-speak that brand-voice.md's Voice Rules explicitly warn against.
