## Economic Trends

- **Small Business Index Q2 2026** (U.S. Chamber of Commerce) — Confidence declined for a second straight quarter; inflation is now cited as the top challenge by 57% of small business owners, up from 53% last quarter and 48% a year ago. Borrowing costs are expected to stay elevated since the Fed is unlikely to cut rates near-term.
  *Why it matters:* At $250K-$4M, clients don't have big-company hedges against rate/inflation swings — this is a credible, specific number to cite when a client says "is it just me feeling this?"
  [Small Business Index Q2 2026 — U.S. Chamber of Commerce](https://www.uschamber.com/small-business/small-business-index-q2-2026)

- **Economic Forecast 2026: Key Opportunities for Small Business** (Forbes / Bill Conerly) — GDP growth projected 4.5-5% nominal in 2026; growth is weaker than prior years but a recession isn't the expectation. Frames 2026 as slower, not falling.
  *Why it matters:* Useful counter-narrative to doom-scrolling clients — "slower, not falling" is a defensible planning stance for scaling decisions rather than freezing.
  [Economic Forecast 2026: Key Opportunities For Small Business](https://www.forbes.com/sites/billconerly/2026/01/28/economic-forecast-2026-key-opportunities-for-small-business/)

## Hiring Strategy

- **Labor Market Outlook for Early 2026** (Robert Half) — "Labor hoarding" is the defining feature: employers aren't cutting staff, but they're also not expanding. Most firms are holding steady rather than making moves in either direction.
  *Why it matters:* Directly relevant to Messy Middle clients weighing a first or second key hire — the market context is "wait and see," which changes how urgent a hire actually is.
  [Labor Market Outlook for Early 2026 Points to a Familiar—But Evolving—Landscape](https://www.roberthalf.com/us/en/insights/research/new-year-labor-market-outlook-and-emerging-trends)

- **The 2026 US Labor Market Is 'Not Collapsing'** (Chase) — NFIB optimism sits above its 52-year average, but "labor quality" — not availability — is now the single most-cited hiring problem. Confidence is up; finding the right person is still hard.
  *Why it matters:* Reframes the hiring conversation from "can I find someone" to "can I find the *right* someone" — matches what Jackie's clients actually complain about in 1:1s.
  [The 2026 US Labor Market Is 'Not Collapsing'](https://www.chase.com/personal/investments/learning-and-insights/article/labor-market-2026-jobs-unemployment-wage-growth-trends)

- **Small Business Employment Trends to Watch in 2026** (Xero) — Practical hiring/staffing strategy guide aimed squarely at small business owners, not enterprise HR.
  *Why it matters:* A source Jackie could point clients to directly, since it's written at their scale rather than a Fortune 500 HR framework.
  [Small Business Employment Trends to Watch in 2026](https://www.xero.com/us/guides/hiring-trends/)

## Growth & Scaling

- **What Scaling Growth Often Exposes In B2B Organizations** (Forbes Councils) — Most B2B scaling failures trace back to weak cash flow models and mismanaged unit economics, not lack of demand. Growth burns capital upfront through hiring and marketing before revenue catches up.
  *Why it matters:* This is the exact failure mode Jackie coaches around in the Messy Middle — "growth" and "healthy" aren't the same thing, and this gives it outside validation.
  [What Scaling Growth Often Exposes In B2B Organizations](https://www.forbes.com/councils/forbesbusinessdevelopmentcouncil/2026/02/26/what-scaling-growth-often-exposes-in-b2b-organizations/)

- **10 Small Business Trends for 2026** (Paychex) — Narrow, workflow-specific tools with clear ROI are outperforming generic software; AI adoption is tracking with rising owner expectations for revenue and staffing.
  *Why it matters:* Backs up a "borrow before you build" / focused-tool argument that fits how Jackie already advises clients on tech spend.
  [10 Small Business Trends for 2026 | Paychex](https://www.paychex.com/articles/management/small-business-trends)
