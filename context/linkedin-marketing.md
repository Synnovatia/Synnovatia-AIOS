# LinkedIn Marketing

> Built 2026-07-12. Governs content, outreach, and engagement on LinkedIn. Voice follows `context/brand-voice.md` throughout — peer not guru, precise not generic, warm not soft, confident not boastful, equip not help.

## Goals

All of the following, in priority order matching `context/strategy.md`:
1. Attract new 1:1/retainer clients (target: 5 new monthly clients in 3 months)

## Ideal Customer Profile (for outreach/prospecting)

- **Role:** Founder, CEO, Owner, President — the actual decision-maker, not an employee
- **Business:** B2B service business
- **Revenue:** $250K–$4M (Stage II / "Messy Middle" per brand positioning) — within that, $250K-$500K skews toward Messy Middle mastermind fit, $1M+ skews toward Seven Figure Forum fit
- **Team size:** Typically 5-10 people (mix of employees/subcontractors)
- **Funding:** Bootstrapped — no VC/PE/angel capital (this is a core brand-voice distinction, not incidental)
- **Signal to look for:** LinkedIn posts/bio language about feeling stuck, stalled growth, "wearing too many hats," wanting outside perspective — this is "Messy Middle" language, the audience's actual felt experience

## Content Pillars & Cadence

**Posts go live 3x/week — Monday, Wednesday, Friday, 7:30am Pacific**, via LinkedIn's own "schedule post" feature (Jackie queues them herself — Claude never posts or schedules anything directly).

**Drafting happens in one batch on Mondays, 7am Pacific** (moved 2026-07-20 from Fridays 9am) — Claude drafts the *following* week's three posts at once, Jackie reviews and schedules them via LinkedIn the same morning. Each post is therefore queued a full week before it goes live.

**Mandatory final pass before presenting any draft (added 2026-07-20, per Jackie):** scrub all AI-tell cadence — "It's not X. It's Y." negation pivots, stacked punchy fragments, anaphoric repetition, "Here's the thing"-style openers, formulaic triads. See the AI-Tell Policy in `context/brand-voice.md`. Jackie should never have to ask for this; drafts arrive already scrubbed.

**Also mandatory, same pass (added 2026-07-27, from observing Jackie's own edits on the Aug 3-7 batch):** push toward blunter, plainer, warmer phrasing before presenting — don't wait for her to edit it out herself.
- **Cut corporate/consultant jargon**, even when accurate: "pivot," "runway," "leverage," "optimize." Swap for the plain word ("lesson learned," "can't get away with that").
- **Cut throat-clearing and meta-explanation.** Don't set up an insight ("I expected X, instead Y") — just state it. Don't explain why a detail matters ("which is where almost every client conversation starts") — cut the clause entirely if the detail already lands on its own.
- **Default shorter.** Jackie's edits consistently collapsed 2-3 sentences into 1, and cut whole paragraphs that elaborated on a point already made. When a paragraph restates or justifies the paragraph before it, cut it.
- **Warmth reads as informal punctuation, not polish.** A well-placed exclamation point, ellipsis, or a rhetorical question answered in a parenthetical ("Can you imagine?! Of course, you can!") is on-voice — don't smooth these out in the name of cleaner prose. This is different from the AI-tell scrub above: formulaic-and-flat gets cut, personal-and-energetic gets kept or even leaned into.
- **Numerals over spelled-out fractions/numbers** in casual context ("1/3" not "a third").
- The client-win pillar in particular tends toward a bigger rewrite than the other two — Jackie will cut an entire reflective/explanatory paragraph and replace it with one plainspoken closing line ("That's a win/win in my book"). Favor economy over thoroughness there especially.
- **Client-win opener pattern (added 2026-08-03, from Jackie's own rewrite of the Aug 14 post):** lead with a direct question to the reader that invites them to self-identify with the problem ("Has this ever happened to you? You spend an entire day creating different prices, scopes, etc. for different potential clients?") rather than opening in third person on the client. Pair it with a short parenthetical caveat acknowledging the situation isn't universally a problem ("For some situations, based on your offerings, that's appropriate.") — this reads as fair-minded rather than absolute, and matches the "confident, not boastful" voice rule. Use this opener structure by default on future client-win drafts, not just when Jackie asks.
- **Closing-question pattern, extended to Story posts (added 2026-08-03, from Jackie's rewrite of the Aug 12 post):** Story posts previously closed on a reflective statement; Jackie's rewrite added a direct engagement question at the very end ("How do you manage the after-meeting ruminating and problem solving?") — same instinct as the Monday thought-leadership closer, now confirmed for Wednesday too. Default to ending every pillar (not just Monday) with a question inviting the reader's own experience, unless the story genuinely lands better on a statement.
- **Self-aware, slightly self-deprecating asides are on-voice (added 2026-08-03):** Jackie's rewrite added "Fortunately or unfortunately..." before a personality trait and closed with the casual idiom "But that's how I roll" — admitting a trait has a mixed side (not purely a strength) reads as more human and less like a curated win. Don't smooth these out; they're warmth, not sloppiness.
- **Cut restating sentences and unnecessary qualifiers even at the cost of a shorter post (reconfirmed 2026-08-03):** Jackie cut "Nothing carried over until the next one landed on the calendar" (restated the prior sentence) and trimmed "her hiring problem" to "her problem" (the specific problem type wasn't load-bearing to the story). When a noun phrase's modifier isn't doing real work, or a sentence just restates the one before it, cut it — don't wait to be asked.

## Outreach / Connection Request Templates

**Template 1 — Cold connection, ICP match found via search/content:**
> Hi [Name] — saw your [post/profile] about [specific detail]. I work with B2B service business owners navigating exactly that stretch — stalled growth, too many hats, ready for a real outside perspective. Would love to connect.

**Template 2 — Warmer, engaged with content first:**
> Hi [Name] — really resonated with what you said about [specific point from their post]. That's the exact "Messy Middle" moment I spend my days helping owners navigate. Would love to connect and follow your journey.

**Template 3 — Mastermind-specific (for Seven Figure Forum / Messy Middle prospects):**
> Hi [Name] — I run two small mastermind groups for B2B service owners around your revenue range ([$1M+ / $250K-$500K]) navigating growth and scaling. No pitch — just wanted to connect given what you're building at [Company].

**Follow-up (after connection accepted, no reply needed to first message):**
> Thanks for connecting, [Name]! Curious — what's the biggest thing on your plate right now as you grow [Company]? Always love hearing how other owners are thinking through the messy middle stretch.

## Commenting/Engagement Guidance

Claude doesn't have live visibility into Jackie's LinkedIn feed. When Jackie shares what she's seeing (a post, a thread), Claude can draft comment angles — but this stays reactive, initiated by Jackie.

**What makes a good comment (per brand voice):**
- Add a specific insight or ask a real question — not "Great post!" generic engagement
- Reference the actual revenue stage/scenario when relevant (precise, not generic)
- Sound like a peer who's been there, not a consultant pitching services

## Hard Boundaries

- **No browser automation on LinkedIn.** Posting, commenting, and connecting are all manual — LinkedIn detects and restricts automated account behavior. This protects Jackie's actual account standing.
- **No auto-posting.** Every draft is reviewed by Jackie before it goes anywhere.
- **Client wins:** never name a client without confirming they're comfortable being named — default to anonymized/generalized.
