Economic Trends (5)

  • Fed Holds Rates Steady, Hawkish Tilt Emerging — CNBC

    The FOMC held its benchmark rate at 3.50–3.75% today, the fifth straight hold, but Governor Waller and others have signaled the committee's focus has flipped from labor-market concern to inflation containment, with chatter about a possible September hike. Why it matters: financing stays parked at current levels a bit longer, but a hawkish pivot means owners planning to draw on a line of credit or finance equipment/expansion should lock in terms now rather than assume rates keep drifting down.

  • NFIB Small Business Optimism Index Rebounds to Four-Month High — NFIB

    The June NFIB Index rose 2.1 points to 97.4, driven mainly by improved expectations for business conditions and real sales — though the Uncertainty Index (89) remains well above its historical average of 68. Why it matters: sentiment among small-business peers and referral sources is turning up on expectations, not current conditions — a useful gut-check for how aggressively to push new business development this quarter versus how much uncertainty is still baked in.

  • June CPI Shows Inflation Cooling, But Core Still Elevated — U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics

    CPI fell 0.4% in June on lower energy prices and is up 3.5% year-over-year; core inflation (ex-food and energy) was flat for the month and up 2.6% over the year. Why it matters: softer headline inflation gives fixed-price service contracts a bit more breathing room near-term, but core costs still running above the Fed's 2% target means software, insurance, and contractor-rate creep likely continues — worth factoring into a 2027 pricing review.

  • Chamber of Commerce Small Business Index: Steady Sentiment, Eroding Cash Comfort — U.S. Chamber of Commerce / Ipsos

    The Q2 2026 Index held roughly steady at 66.5, but inflation concern jumped to 57% of owners (from 48% a year ago) and fewer owners report being "very comfortable" with their cash flow than last quarter. Why it matters: the gap between stable headline sentiment and quietly tightening cash comfort is exactly the tension bootstrapped $250K–$4M owners feel — a good prompt to revisit AR terms and cash buffers before Q4 rather than trust the topline number.

  • Atlanta Fed Cuts Q2 GDP Growth Estimate Sharply — Federal Reserve Bank of Atlanta, GDPNow

    The GDPNow model's Q2 2026 growth estimate has fallen to 1.6%, down from 3.0% in mid-June, tracking close to Q1's 1.6% pace as more data came in through July. Why it matters: the broader economy appears to be downshifting even though Q1 looked resilient on the surface — service firms selling into other small or mid-size businesses should watch for softer demand signals from clients in more cyclical sectors.
