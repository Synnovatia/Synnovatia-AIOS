What I'm Watching

Buyer Sentiment & Behavior (4)

  • WSJ/Vistage Small Business CEO Confidence Index — Vistage

    The July 2026 survey of Vistage CEO members ($1M–$20M revenue) shows confidence rising again — the share who see the economy as worse than a year ago fell to 34%, down from 48% the prior month, as owners adjust pricing to protect margins. Why it matters: this revenue band is a close proxy for your clients' own customers — less defensiveness here suggests prospects may be more open to investment conversations right now than they were earlier this year.

  • NFIB Small Business Optimism Index — NFIB

    The Index jumped to 97.4 in June (released July 14), nearing its 52-year average, driven by improved sales and business-condition expectations — though inflation remains the #1 reported challenge. Why it matters: optimism is genuinely improving, but cost pressure hasn't eased — owners in your range are willing to invest again, but they'll scrutinize ROI harder than they would in a low-inflation environment, so proof and payback period need to be explicit in pitches.

  • Small Business Index Q2 2026 — U.S. Chamber of Commerce

    The overall index held steady at 66.5, but the share of owners citing inflation as a top challenge climbed to 57% (from 48% a year ago), and positive views of the local economy fell eight points year-over-year even as national-economy views held up better. Why it matters: national and local outlook are diverging for small business owners — when selling into a specific local or regional service business, the client's mood tends to track their own neighborhood more than the headlines.

  • The Surprising Economics of B2B Growth — McKinsey Global B2B Pulse Survey

    McKinsey's survey of nearly 4,000 B2B decision-makers across 13 countries finds buyers now use an average of ten channels across a purchase and spend only a small fraction of the journey in direct contact with vendors, doing most research and validation on their own. Why it matters: for a solo practice with no marketing department, this is the core strategic implication — prospects are forming an opinion before they ever talk to you, so the website, content, and third-party proof have to carry more of the persuasion load than a sales conversation can.

Growth & Marketing Tactics (4)

  • The Top Marketing Channels of 2026, According to Marketers — HubSpot

    HubSpot's 2026 survey of 1,500+ marketers ranks website/blog/SEO as the top ROI channel, followed by paid and organic social, with email still delivering the highest return per dollar of any channel measured. Why it matters: without a paid-media budget, this validates doubling down on an owned asset (site plus content) and an email nurture sequence rather than chasing paid-social trends — the channels a one-person practice can realistically sustain are the ones ranking best anyway.

  • New B2B Research: Winning Marketing Teams Are Building Fundamentals, Not Just Playing With Prompts — Content Marketing Institute

    CMI's 2026 B2B study (1,000+ marketers, with MarketingProfs) finds 95% of B2B marketers now use AI in their workflow, but only about 39% say it's actually improving performance — the gap is in strategy and follow-through, not tool access. Why it matters: clients are likely already dabbling in AI content tools — this reframes the advice from "use AI" to "AI without a documented strategy and consistent execution won't move the needle," a useful contrarian point to raise with clients tempted to treat AI as a shortcut.

  • AI SEO Statistics and Insights for 2026 — Position

    Traffic referred from AI platforms (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, etc.) is converting up to 4.4x better than traditional organic search, and overall AI search traffic is up 527% year-over-year — but the platforms increasingly keep users rather than sending clicks to websites. Why it matters: a small service business can't outspend anyone on traditional SEO, but a small volume of highly-converting AI-search visitors is a real, low-cost opportunity — worth a quick check on whether a client's site content is even structured to be cited by AI answer engines.

  • B2B Referral / Word-of-Mouth Research — Wynter

    Wynter's survey of B2B SaaS CMOs found 42% rank word-of-mouth as the top channel that gets a vendor into consideration, versus just 2% each for paid ads and cold outreach; separately, referral-driven revenue reportedly exceeds 60% of the business for many consultants and professional-services providers. Why it matters: useful ammunition for justifying time spent on referral systems — proactively asking happy clients, building a couple of simple partner relationships — over paid acquisition, which this data says underperforms for exactly this type of business.
