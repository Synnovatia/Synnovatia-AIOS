What I'm Watching

Economic Trends (4)

  • Fed Holds Rates Steady at 3.50%–3.75%, but Vote Was Divided 9-3 — CNBC / NPR

    The FOMC held its benchmark rate steady on July 29, but three regional presidents dissented, arguing for a hike given inflation has stayed above the 2% target for five-plus years, and markets now expect one to two more hikes by year-end. Why it matters: a divided Fed signals rates aren't coming down soon and could still rise — worth flagging to clients financing growth or holding variable-rate debt, and a reason to keep pricing/cash-reserve conversations going rather than waiting for relief.

  • CPI Fell 0.4% in June, but Core Inflation Still Up 2.6% Year-Over-Year — U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics

    Headline CPI dipped on a seasonally adjusted basis after May's 0.5% rise, but the all-items index is still up 3.5% over the past year, with core (ex food/energy) inflation running at 2.6%. Why it matters: input costs (software, contractors, insurance) are still creeping up even as headline numbers look better — a good prompt to revisit whether client service contracts have inflation-adjustment or annual-increase clauses built in.

  • Q2 GDP Growth Slowed to 1.5%, Down From 2.1% in Q1 — U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis

    The advance estimate shows consumer spending, investment, and exports drove growth, partly offset by lower government spending and rising imports, but the pace is clearly decelerating from Q1. Why it matters: a cooling economy tends to hit B2B service demand with a lag — if clients sell into industries sensitive to overall business investment, this is an early signal to watch pipeline and renewal conversations more closely over the next two quarters.

  • Small Business Profitability Turned Positive in June for the First Time This Year — Bank of America Institute

    BofA's account-data checkpoint shows profitability growth turning positive, though revenue growth still isn't fully offsetting cost pressures, and owners have been planning price increases for six-plus years running. Why it matters: this is a real-time read (not a lagging survey) on the margin squeeze the client base is living through — useful ammunition for a "yes, you can still raise prices" conversation, since peers are doing the same thing.

Growth & Scaling (4)

  • Goldman Sachs: Small Businesses Embrace AI But Need Training to Fully Harness It — Goldman Sachs

    Goldman's latest small-business survey finds 76% of small businesses now using AI in some capacity and 93% reporting a positive business impact, but only 14% have fully integrated it into core operations, and the top barrier for non-adopters is simply not seeing a use case. Why it matters: the adoption-vs-integration gap is exactly where a $250K–$4M service firm loses money — a strong hook for a "where in your delivery process could this actually change unit economics" conversation with clients.

  • Professional Services M&A Activity Up 13% Year-Over-Year in Q1 2026, Led by PE-Backed Accounting Roll-Ups — FOCUS Investment Banking / Cherry Bekaert

    129 U.S. professional services deals were announced in Q1, driven largely by private-equity-fueled consolidation in accounting, tax, and advisory; roughly 90 PE-backed accounting deals are expected to close in 2026, though the pool of easy targets is shrinking. Why it matters: consolidation in adjacent professional-services niches is a leading indicator for where B2B service buyers — and competitors — are headed; also a reminder that a well-run $1–4M firm with clean financials is an increasingly attractive acquisition target.

  • PwC: Sustainable Growth Requires Disciplined Resource Allocation and a Willingness to Divest — PwC

    PwC's mid-year industrials-and-services outlook finds that companies managing growth well are the ones actively reviewing and reallocating investment — and cutting underperforming lines — rather than just adding more. Why it matters: for a solo-practitioner-scale firm, this is a useful counter to the instinct to keep adding services/offers to chase revenue; it backs up a "what should you stop doing" conversation as much as a "what should you start doing" one.

  • Chase 2026 Business Leaders Outlook: 59% Now See AI as Essential to Competitiveness Within Three Years — JPMorganChase

    Despite lingering recession worry (down to 27% from 39% mid-year) and persistent inflation/tariff concerns, 74% of business leaders are optimistic about 2026, and a majority are increasing marketing/tech investment and building cash reserves. Why it matters: "optimistic but building cash reserves and investing in tech" is precisely the posture a strategic advisor wants clients to take right now — validates a balanced growth-plus-resilience message rather than either pure caution or pure aggression.
