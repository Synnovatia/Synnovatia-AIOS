A practice that fits in three hours a day

Synnovatia is a solo strategic-consulting practice for bootstrapped B2B service businesses ($250K–$4M), delivering 1:1 consulting, monthly retainers, and two masterminds. Its founder runs the business on about three hours a day while carrying roughly twenty hours a week of graduate coursework — so recovered time is the entire point of the EVOLV-OS build, not a nice-to-have.

In just over two weeks the workspace has stood up four layers — Context, Data, Intelligence, and the Automate layer now actively running — inventoried around thirty recurring business tasks, and systematized twenty-three of them. What follows is what that actually means in practice.

The Three KPIs

How we know it's working

The program measures three things. A note on the first: a task counts as systematized when a scheduled job or repeatable process now carries the mechanical work. Where a human still reviews and sends — every client email is draft-only — that is a deliberate design choice, not an unfinished one. The drafting, tracking, and timing are offloaded; the judgment and the send stay human on purpose.

The Automate Layer

What's been built

Grouped by what it does. Everything below runs today — the daily systems unattended, the client-facing and marketing systems draft-only for review.

Data & Intelligence

Runs daily · unattended

The numbers and the morning picture, gathered before the day starts.

  • Live business metrics — Stripe revenue collected every morning at 6am; HubSpot on demand. Was: manual dashboard-checking.
  • The Dashboard — business + personal snapshot rebuilt and republished each morning to a stable link. Was: 5–10 min/day across three sources.
  • Morning brief — weekday 7:10am gather of calendar and unanswered email, rendered to read.
  • Meeting intelligence — every Zoom auto-records and auto-summarizes; any past meeting is searchable. Was: 10–15 min/meeting of notes.

Client lifecycle

Draft-only · human sends

From first welcome to long-dormant reconnection, drafted and tracked — never sent without review.

  • Onboarding — detects new Closed-Won deals, drafts the welcome, tracks the agreement/profile/scheduling/invoice checklist, nudges if it stalls. Was: 20–30 min/client.
  • Pre-meeting objective & post-meeting recap — calendar-detected for 1:1 clients; drafts the objective ask days out and a same-day recap with action items from the Zoom summary.
  • Client re-engagement — a 174-client roster on a 6-month cadence. Each Monday: check replies, refresh the due list, draft five personalized reconnections with a priority queue for warm contacts.
The proof. On July 22 this system produced the practice's first re-engagement conversion — a client last active in 2011, reached by a cold email on July 6 and signed for book coaching sixteen days later.

Marketing

Draft-only · on a calendar

A content operation that keeps cadence without keeping the founder at the keyboard.

  • LinkedIn — three posts batch-drafted each Monday for the following week, in brand voice, with an automatic pass to scrub "AI-tell" cadence before she sees them.
  • HubSpot email — three segments (Active Engagers, Drifting, Lapsed), each on its own cadence, now with a performance log that benchmarks every send's open, click, and unsubscribe rates.
  • Mastermind growth — a reusable cohort-launch checklist, a twice-monthly candidate-pipeline review, and a roster-mining pass that surfaced nine qualified prospects across the two groups.

Operations & personal

Reminders · logs

The recurring obligations that quietly eat a week, put on rails.

  • Bookkeeping, bill-pay & reconciliation — reminders aligned to real bill-arrival dates, spanning the practice, household, and a spouse's business. Money movement stays human by hard rule.
  • Home & health — every recurring service (HVAC, pest, tree, granite, spa, garden, windows), the annual physical and eye exam, the weekly weigh-in, and the workout program on automated reminders or logs.

Principles in Practice

What we learned building it

The program's principles held up under real use — here is where each earned its keep.

Borrow before you build

The re-engagement system was migrated from an existing build, not re-created; one mastermind-reminder pattern was reused across twenty dated tasks.

Human-in-the-loop by default

Every client and marketing email is draft-only; money never moves without the founder. That's why the system can be trusted to run unattended.

Reject fragile automation honestly

Browser-automating LinkedIn posting and grocery-sale scanning were tried and rejected as unreliable. A reliable reminder beat a fragile robot.

Verify before acting

A pre-outreach research pass caught a former client with a federal wire-fraud conviction before a warm email went out — a save the raw data never would have surfaced.

The data narrows; the human decides

The CRM's revenue field was wrong on every contact checked, and retirements were invisible everywhere. The tools shortlist; the founder's knowledge is the filter.

Layers, not leaps

Four layers in sixteen days, each independently useful the day it shipped — no big-bang rebuild, no month of setup before the first payoff.
