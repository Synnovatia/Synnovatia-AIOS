## Monday, August 3 — Thought Leadership

In 27 years of consulting across industries, there's one major difference between venture-backed founders and bootstrapped owners: how they treat a wrong decision. A funded founder can spend six months finding out something doesn't work and call it a lesson learned. An owner in the $250K–$4M range can't get away with that — every hire and dollar has to be closer to right the first time.

This constraint actually sharpens judgment more than it slows people down. The strongest owners I work with in the Messy Middle aren't the best-resourced — they're the ones who've had the least margin for error and learned to use it.

If you've built without outside capital, what's the best instinct that "bootstrapping" gave you? I'd bet you already know the answer.

## Wednesday, August 5 — Story

I'm back in school now, working toward a degree in cultural anthropology — which means most weeks I'm the least experienced person in the room again, still guessing at what the professor wants. It's an odd thing to feel after 25+ years advising owners on how to run their businesses.

It's turned out to be useful. It reminds me what it feels like to be stuck without knowing why....very much like those I work with! Turns out anthropology and consulting share the same core skill: noticing the patterns people can't see because they're too close to their own situation. Funny how that works, whether you're studying it or living it!

## Friday, August 7 — Client Win

Recently a client turned away 1/3 of the leads coming in — deliberately! (Can you imagine?! Of course, you can!) After having spent close to two years saying yes to everyone, she no longer had the capacity to deliver at the level she wanted for her clients.

Here's what's most interesting — and why most owners don't turn away leads earlier — Revenue didn't drop — it went up, because the clients who remained were the ones her business was actually built for. And even better, her stress went way down. That's a win/win in my book.
