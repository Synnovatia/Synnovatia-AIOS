## Writing-style patterns from this edit (logged to memory and `context/linkedin-marketing.md`)

- **Question openers to the reader on Monday, not just the old Client Win pillar.** "Have you ever wondered why...?" replaced the client-scenario opener on Monday, and Friday opens the same way ("Have you ever wondered why people choose to work with you? I did!").
- **Closing on a suggested rule or takeaway is fine.** Monday ends on the fix and Wednesday on "Maybe we need a new rule for our business too…". The question moved up front (Monday's opener, Wednesday's "don't we?") instead of sitting at the end. The question-close default still holds, but it isn't mandatory when the post opens on one.
- **Precise terms in personal stories.** "Shoulder and hip" became "anterior deltoid and piriformis." She prefers the exact word even in a casual story.
- **Playful self-cheering and self-teasing asides:** "(Yah me!)", "(other than me!)", plus the idiom "slow my roll." Same family as Egads!/Oy! and "But that's how I roll."
- **Fair-minded parenthetical caveat reconfirmed:** "(Okay – so there is that element of costs going up but hopefully you're keeping up with price adjustments.)" Same move as "For some situations... that's appropriate."
- **Tag question for inclusive framing:** "We experience the same urge, don't we?"
- **Broader, simpler diagnosis over a structural prescription.** The Monday fix went from "team leaders who own the numbers" to "removing unnecessary complexity and updating processes." She rewrote it plainer and more general, and much shorter (two paragraphs).
- **Real specifics over summaries in the series:** the two actual interview questions ("why did you hire me? Why do you continue to hire me?") replace "a handful of open questions." Plus an explicit "Follow along!" ask, which speaks to the follower-growth goal behind the series.
- **More confident framing:** "the first time in a while the progress feels like it'll stick" became "the progress will stick."

> ## Monday, October 5 — Thought Leadership
>
> An owner I talked with recently, running a business right around $3M, told me revenue was up again this year and they were taking home less than they did two years ago.
>
> That's common at the $3M–$4M stage, and the reason is usually the same. The business has outgrown one person's judgment, but nobody else is responsible for the numbers yet. Every decision still runs through the owner, so small costs pile up where no one's watching: a project that runs over budget, a discount nobody tracked. None of them looks big enough to stop and fix. >
