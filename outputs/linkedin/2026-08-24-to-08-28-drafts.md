## Monday, August 24 — Thought Leadership

When you're overwhelmed, the advice is always the same: do a time audit. Track every 15 minutes for a week and see where it's going.

I think that's the wrong move.

A time audit just confirms what you probably already suspected — too many meetings, too much firefighting, not enough of the work that actually grows the business. Confirming the problem doesn't fix it.

What actually helps is building a framework first. How do you want your week to look if nothing was on fire? Which hours are for client work, which are for the business itself, which are protected no matter what.

Once you have that framework, the audit becomes useful. Now you're comparing your real week against the one you decided on, not just cataloguing chaos for its own sake.

If you run a $250K–$4M service business, you probably don't have an extra 10 hours hiding somewhere. You have the same 40, spent differently.

How would you actually want to spend your week, if you got to decide first instead of reacting your way through it?
