## Monday, September 28 — Thought Leadership

An owner in the $750K–$1M range showed me their onboarding document last month, proud of it, and they should be. It covers everything.

Then they told me how it actually goes. A new hire starts maybe twice a year, so every time, they end up walking the person through it from memory while the document sits in a folder.

Written down and ready to hand off turn out to be two different finish lines. Most of us stop at the first one.

What's something in your business that's written down but still really lives in your head?

## Wednesday, September 30 — Story

A client spent a good chunk of a session last month venting about a team member who kept missing deadlines. I let them (some weeks that's exactly what the hour is for).

Then I asked what they wanted to do about it. There was a long pause, and then they told me they'd already decided. They needed to say all of it out loud first, to someone who wouldn't tell them they were overreacting.

We spent the rest of the session on the conversation they'd been dreading: what to say and how to say it.

How do you know when it's time to stop venting and start deciding?

## Friday, October 2 — Ongoing Story Series, Part 3

Part 3: how I actually figured out what makes Synnovatia different (a story in installments)

Where we left off: no single answer explained why clients picked me. Two did, together. How it feels to work with me, and two kinds of knowledge behind the advice, one wide and one deep.

Then one client said there's no one else doing what I do, at this level, for solopreneurs. Most strategic advice is built for funded startups or companies with a full leadership team. Bootstrapped owners in the $250K–$4M range usually get neither kind.

That comment is a big reason the Fortune 500 line ended up in my positioning.

Next week: my first try at putting all of this into one sentence, and why it fell flat.

Where do you go for advice built for a business your size?
