## Monday, September 21 — Thought Leadership

A client told me last month she was waiting for the economy to feel more stable before she made her next hire.

Confidence keeps drifting — up a little, down a little, never settling anywhere close to what anyone would call stable. If you're waiting for that number to hold still, you could be waiting years.

I wrote about this on the blog a few weeks ago: "Waiting for the economy to stabilize before you grow isn't caution — it's the single most common reason a $250K–$4M service business stays stuck." The businesses making their growth call now, uncertainty and all, are outpacing the ones still waiting for a green light that isn't coming.

What decision have you been sitting on until things feel more certain?

## Wednesday, September 23 — Story

A client asked me a specific licensing question in the middle of a session once — something about their particular industry I'd genuinely never run into before.

I told them I didn't know, and I wasn't going to guess. I spent that evening finding someone who actually did, and called them back the next morning with a real answer instead of a confident-sounding wrong one.

They told me later that was the moment they trusted me with the bigger stuff — not because I had every answer, but because I didn't pretend to.

Have you ever trusted someone more after they admitted they didn't know something?

## Friday, September 25 — Ongoing Story Series, Part 2

Part 2: how I actually figured out what makes Synnovatia different (a story in installments)

Last week: I sat down with eight clients and one outside advisor, one at a time, and asked open questions expecting a real answer. Nobody gave me the same one.

When I laid all the notes out side by side, two separate patterns did show up. One was about how it feels to work with me — clients kept describing feeling genuinely heard, talking to someone who's actually run a business instead of studying one from the outside. The other was about the thinking itself — people kept naming two different kinds of knowledge at once: the pattern recognition that comes from working across a lot of industries, and the depth that comes from actually understanding their specific situation.

Neither one alone explained why people picked me over anyone else. Together, they did.

That's the moment the positioning actually changed — not because I found one big insight, but because I stopped looking for one.

Next week: the specific conversation that showed me just how rare that combination actually is.

What would surprise you more — finding out your best clients all say the same thing, or finding out they don't?
