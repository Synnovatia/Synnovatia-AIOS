## Monday, August 17 — Thought Leadership

Most advisors specialize in one lane. They've either built deep expertise in a single industry, or they can talk broadly about business in general but nothing specific enough to be useful in your actual situation.

In 27 years of consulting across industries in the $250K–$4M range, I've noticed the owners who get the most out of outside advice want both — someone who's seen enough different businesses to spot the pattern, and someone who'll get specific enough about their industry to be useful in the room.

That combination is rare — most consultants pick a lane early and stay in it.

Which do you actually need more right now: someone who's seen the pattern before, or someone who knows your industry cold?

## Wednesday, August 19 — Story

A client's website was getting steady traffic and going nowhere. Good copy, clear offer, still almost no one filled out the contact form.

We rewrote the headline. No change. We added testimonials. Small bump, nothing real. On the third pass, we finally looked at what her actual clients said when they signed — almost every one of them mentioned a phone call before they filled out anything online. Turned out the sticking point wasn't the form itself — it was asking people to commit before they'd talked to a real person.

We swapped the button to "Schedule a call" instead of "Send a message." Inquiries picked up within two weeks.

I'm a bit of a dog with a bone about problems like this — once something's stuck, I can't let it go until it clicks. Ask my husband.

What's a problem you tried to fix a few different ways before you found what was actually wrong?

## Friday, August 21 — Client Win

Has this ever happened to you? You know exactly what you want to do next in your business, and you just don't do it? (Sometimes that hesitation is worth listening to — it means something isn't ready yet.)

A client of mine had been sitting on a new service line for over a year. She knew who wanted it and roughly what to charge. She just hadn't picked a date.

We spent one session turning the idea into an actual plan: launch in six weeks, three people to tell first, one intro email drafted before she left the call.

She launched on schedule. Her first month alone beat what she'd projected for the whole quarter. All she'd needed was a deadline.
