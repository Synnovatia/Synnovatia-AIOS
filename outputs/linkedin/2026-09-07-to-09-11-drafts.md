## Monday, September 7 — Thought Leadership

A lot of advice for business owners is really just encouragement in disguise, like "believe in yourself" or "trust the process." That's not wrong. It just doesn't tell you what to do on Tuesday when a client wants a refund and payroll is due Friday. Egads!

What IS helpful at the $250K–$4M stage is a second brain: someone who looks at the real data with you and helps you decide what happens next.

What's the most helpful piece of business advice you've ever gotten?

## Wednesday, September 9 — Story

You might know that I've been conducting "different is better than better" research. Along with that, I've been updating my website copy (fingers crossed, it'll be ready soon). It's been quite the process. Every draft sounded almost right and also off, and I couldn't tell you why.

I finally sent it to a colleague who does nothing but brand positioning. She read it once and pointed out what I was missing. I'd been too close to my own business to see that!

I coach owners through exactly this kind of blind spot for a living and I still needed someone else's eyes on my own. Oy!

What's something in your business you couldn't see clearly until someone else pointed it out?

## Friday, September 11 — What I'm Watching

Here's an intriguing stat I came across this week: B2B companies attribute 54% of their pipeline to referrals and word of mouth. Companies with an actual formal referral process see 86% more revenue growth over two years than the ones without one (Heinz Marketing, 2026).

Despite that, few of us have anything close to a real system for it. Do you have an actual referral process, or does it happen only when a client thinks to bring it up?

**What changed, and what it confirms or adds to `feedback_linkedin-post-editing-style.md` / `feedback_jackie-native-writing-voice.md`:**

- **New interjection words, same established device.** "Egads!" (Monday) and "Oy!" (Wednesday) — two new specific exclamatory interjections dropped in as standalone personality beats, extending the already-documented pattern (previously: "Can you imagine?!", "Let's face it.") with fresh, distinctly old-fashioned/wry word choices. Worth drawing from this specific vocabulary family (Egads, Oy) rather than defaulting to generic ones going forward.
- **Splits a comma+"but" sentence into two short sentences, not just em-dash joins.** "That's not wrong, but it doesn't tell you..." → "That's not wrong. It just doesn't tell you..." The previously-documented em-dash-split habit extends to comma-conjunction joins too — general instinct toward shorter, separated sentences over one longer joined one.
- **Reframes a negative closing question into a positive one.** "What's the most useless piece of business advice you've ever gotten?" → "What's the most helpful piece of business advice you've ever gotten?" Even though the post's setup criticizes generic advice, she chose to close on an invitation to share something positive rather than pile onto the criticism. Worth defaulting closing questions toward a positive/constructive frame even when the post body is more critical.
- **All-caps single-word emphasis, a new typographic device.** "What IS helpful" — capitalizing one word for vocal stress. Not previously logged; distinct from italics or exclamation points as an emphasis tool.
- **Anchors a story to a real, nameable project instead of a vague timeframe.** "this summer" → explicit references to the actual "Different Is Better Than Better" research and the real website-copy project, plus a hopeful parenthetical ("fingers crossed, it'll be ready soon"). When a real, current, nameable initiative exists, she'll pull it in by name rather than keeping a story generic — a concrete extension of "precise, not generic."
- **When a flagged-as-illustrative specific detail isn't confirmed, she vagues it rather than inventing a replacement specific.** The draft's specific (but flagged-as-invented) detail — "I was describing what I do instead of the problem I solve" — became the vaguer "pointed out what I was missing" rather than a different invented specific. Useful going forward: when a Story detail is flagged as unconfirmed, it's safer to leave it appropriately vague in the draft than to guess a plausible-sounding specific.
- **"Here's a/an [descriptor] stat/thing I came across" reconfirmed on-voice for a stat-intro opener specifically**, a second data point after the 7/31 batch ("Here's what's most interesting"). This structurally resembles the "Here's the thing"-style AI-tell the cadence-scrub rule flags, but in both confirmed instances she's used it herself to introduce a stat/finding — a narrower, genuine exception to the general "avoid 'Here's the thing' openers" rule, not a reason to abandon that rule elsewhere.
- **Heavy-cut instinct on the Friday pillar now confirmed to carry over from Client Win to What I'm Watching.** The original draft's second and third paragraphs (a specific list of missing-process examples, plus a prescriptive "ask/make it easy/follow up" close) were cut down to one linking sentence: "Despite that, few of us have anything close to a real system for it." Previously this "favor economy over thoroughness" guidance was tied specifically to the old Client Win pillar — now confirmed to apply to What I'm Watching too.
- **New: switches from third-person "owners I talk to" to inclusive first-person-plural "us."** "Few [owners] have..." → "few of us have..." A direct, concrete implementation of "peer, not guru" — she includes herself in the audience's gap rather than describing it from outside. Worth defaulting to "we/us" over third-person "owners" when describing a shared struggle or gap, distinct from the existing "we/our vs. you/your" rule in `brand-voice.md` (which is about general-passage vs. direct-instruction mode) — this is specifically about inclusive framing of a shared problem.
- Reconfirms the existing "drop the redundant comma before and/because" pattern ("for a living and I still needed," no comma) and the existing "add exclamation points at emotional beats" pattern (three instances across two posts this batch).
