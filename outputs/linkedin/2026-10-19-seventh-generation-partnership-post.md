Have you ever assumed your business partner saw things the same way you did?

Jeffrey Hollender and Alan Newman built Seventh Generation together. Hollender was good at raising money, and Newman ran operations and marketing. Unilever bought the company in 2016 for a reported $700 million.

Newman was long gone by then. During the early 1990s recession, worn out from years of long hours, he took a sabbatical and expected to come back. Hollender, left running a struggling business, felt abandoned. They never wrote down what the leave meant, and they didn't talk while Newman was away. Hollender eventually told him he was out.

A few weeks ago I shared the partnership checklist I wrote in 2010. Two of its questions fit this story almost exactly. What happens if a partner is absent? Whose decision wins in a tie or a dispute? (Looking back, both founders said they each wanted to be in charge.)

If you have a business partner, have you talked through what happens if one of you needs to step away?
