## Focus for both — tied directly to the pricing story

Not "what's Jackie like" (you already have plenty of warmth-proof-points banked). The gap right now is **value-for-investment**, said by a client, in their own words. Every question below is built to surface one of three things:

1. **The before/after** — what was actually different about their decision-making or business trajectory because of the work
2. **The objectivity/clarity gap** — both already independently validated this theme in the original research (Mark: "I was too close to see my own situation objectively"; this is your single strongest proof point for *why an outside strategic view is worth paying for*)
3. **Would-they-pay-it-again** — the most direct pricing-relevant question there is, asked naturally rather than as a hard sell

## Recording logistics — keep it low-friction

Neither of them is a videographer and this shouldn't feel like a production:

- **Self-recorded on their phone, landscape orientation, in a quiet room with decent light** (facing a window works better than overhead light). No equipment needed.
- **Length: 60-90 seconds is the target**, even if they record longer — you'll edit down. Tell them "short answers, don't overthink it" up front so they don't over-prepare into something stiff.
- **Alternative if phone-recording feels awkward for either of them:** a quick Fathom-recorded Zoom call where you ask the questions live and pull the video from the recording. This trades a little more of your time for a much higher chance they actually follow through — worth offering as the easy option rather than assuming self-recording is always preferred.
- Ask them to send the raw file however's easiest (email, Google Drive link, text) — don't make the logistics the reason it doesn't happen.

## The prompts — send these ahead of time

Keep to 3, so the video stays tight. Send as a simple list, framed as "answer whichever ones feel natural, in your own words":

1. **What was actually going on in your business before we started working together?**
   *(Surfaces the before-state — the "too close to see it objectively" moment, in their own words this time.)*
2. **What's different now because of that outside perspective?**
   *(The ROI/value question, without asking "was it worth the money" directly — a specific decision, a specific shift in how they think, is more credible than a general compliment.)*
3. **Would you tell another business owner at your stage that this was worth the investment?**
   *(The one question that speaks directly to a prospect weighing the new price — let them answer it in their own words rather than putting words in their mouth.)*
