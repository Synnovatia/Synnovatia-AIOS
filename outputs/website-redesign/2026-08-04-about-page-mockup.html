Strategy built for owners, for the growth stage that doesn't come with instructions.

Stage II entrepreneurs don't need another program. They need a strategist who does the hard work: deep engagement, sustained effort, and a willingness to go where other advisors stop.

Jackie Nagel Founder, Synnovatia

Why I started Synnovatia

I started Synnovatia because I believe small business owners deserve the same quality of strategic thinking that Fortune 500 companies take for granted, and I wanted to do something about it.

What I've found over 25+ years of working with Stage II entrepreneurs (business owners navigating roughly $250,000–$4M in revenue) is that the problem is rarely what it looks like on the surface. There's almost always a gap between the symptom you're experiencing and the core issue driving it. That's exactly what I built Synnovatia to address.

I'm passionate about two things: small business and science-based performance strategies. Together, they let us build an approach that goes beyond your business goals, to how you actually operate, because the two are more connected than most people realize.

And I believe in walking the talk. I test and iterate strategies in my own business. When something stops working, I stop. When something new is worth trying, I try it. My clients deserve an advisor who models the same adaptability she coaches.

Strategic thinking, plus what it actually takes to achieve it.

The View You Can't Get From Inside

Clarity that only comes from outside

When you're running a growing business, you are too close to see it clearly. Clients consistently describe this as the turning point: finally seeing their situation from the outside. That shift in perspective is what makes everything else actionable.

The Peer Dynamic That Makes It Land

A fellow business owner, not a detached expert

The advice carries weight because it's grounded in lived experience: my own years in business, working with owners across industries and stages. Clients experience me as someone who understands their business, not a coach selling a methodology.

Where Others Stop, I Don't

Sleeves rolled up, not pom-poms raised

I'm encouraging and supportive, but that's not the same as cheerleading. I engage with the actual problem, work through it collaboratively, and deliver something concrete. That persistence is what clients point to as fundamentally different from other advisors they've worked with.

The Emotional Intelligence That Changes the Work

Space for what's actually happening

Stage II is isolating. You carry things you can't say to your team, your family, or your competitors. I hold space for the ache and frustration of building a business, because the emotional reality of ownership is part of the strategic reality, not separate from it.

The Methodology That Sharpens the Intuition

Science-based strategy that makes instinct more precise

Intuition matters, but it's most powerful when it's grounded in science. I use evidence-based strategy to uncover what's actually driving the problem, not just what's visible on the surface. That rigor is what turns a good instinct into a permanent solution.

The Longevity That Proves the Value

Clients who stay, some for over a decade

The length of a relationship is its own data point. Clients who have worked with me since 2000, 2011, and 2014 aren't renewing a contract. They're continuing a relationship that proves its value repeatedly over time. That's the proof I'm most proud of.

My approach

The Messy Middle is a specific place: past early-stage chaos, but well short of the plateau of a mature business. It's the stage where the strategies that got you here aren't getting you there: growth creates complexity faster than you can manage it, and you're simultaneously too big to operate intuitively and too small to hire the talent that would solve it.

I don't package clients into programs. I listen, ask different questions, and together we develop options calibrated to where you actually are and what's achievable for you to implement, not where a curriculum assumes you should be.

And I keep thinking about your problems between sessions. When our appointment ends, I may physically clock out, but I don't mentally clock out, and that's often when the real problem-solving happens.
