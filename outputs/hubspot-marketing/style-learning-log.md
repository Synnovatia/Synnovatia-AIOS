**2026-08-27 — Active Engagers — "Email: Active Engagers (09/02/26)" — live in-editor diff, not a post-send comparison.** Jackie hand-edited Draft 1 directly in the HubSpot editor same-day, before send — pulled via `GET_CONTENT`/`GET_EMAIL_DETAILS` and compared against the version Claude last set. Unlike the two entries above, this is a real edit of the Claude draft, not a full rewrite — most of the structure and both stats survived.

- **Subject rewritten from a declarative to a question-plus-confident-answer:** "The number behind where your next client is probably coming from" → "Where is your next client coming from? We might know." Extends the self-Q&A device already logged in `feedback_jackie-native-writing-voice` (previously seen mid-body, e.g. "So, what can you do now? I'm so glad you asked.") to subject lines specifically — a new context for an existing pattern, not a new pattern.
- **Dropped the "$250K-$4M" revenue-range figure in favor of "B2B service businesses."** "Most $250K-$4M service businesses already grow this way..." → "Most B2B service businesses already grow this way..." This is the **second confirmed instance** of the exact edit logged as pattern 11 in `feedback_whats-watching-email-edit-patterns` (2026-08-27, Drifting WIW format) — that entry called it a one-data-point, format-specific exception to `brand-voice.md`'s "name the revenue range" rule. It just repeated in a *different* email format (direct-CTA + stat-nod, not a pure WIW value email) on the same day. Worth treating as a confirmed HubSpot-email-body pattern now, not WIW-format-specific — see the updated note in that memory file.
- **Collapsed a three-item enumerated list into one open-ended parenthetical.** "...or a pricing conversation, or a hire you keep circling, I do solutions-on-the-fly calls for exactly that. 30 or 60 minutes, no retainer, no pitch." → "...(or anything else keeping you awake at night) I provide 30 - 60 minute **solutions-on-the-fly** calls for exactly that." Cut the enumerated examples and the "no retainer, no pitch" reassurance line entirely, replaced with a single vivid catch-all phrase. New pattern, distinct from the already-logged "cuts elaboration in favor of one plain sentence" instinct — this specifically swaps a *list of examples* for one idiomatic catch-all rather than just trimming.
- **Bolded the named service inline.** "solutions-on-the-fly" is now bold in the body copy — the first time a service name (as opposed to a proper noun like "Mastermind for the Messy Middle") has gotten inline emphasis rather than just being named exactly. Worth watching for repetition before treating as standing style.
- **Replaced the credibility/positioning close with a short metaphor.** "Just an outside read from someone who's spent 25+ years watching businesses at this stage work through the same stuck points." → "Just a quick infusion of clarity to keep you moving forward." Drops the explicit "25+ years" experience callback entirely in favor of a punchier, benefit-first metaphor ("infusion of clarity") not seen in any prior logged sample. Flag to Jackie whether this is a deliberate move away from credibility-framing in short/casual sends, or just this line — one data point.
- **Real technical finding, not a voice edit — manual paragraph-spacer workaround.** Jackie inserted an empty `<p>&nbsp;</p>` between every paragraph. Root cause: HubSpot's own base email CSS sets `p{margin:0}` (confirmed via `PREVIEW_CONTENT`'s rendered HTML), so consecutive `<p>` tags in an `EmailTextModule` render with zero visual gap between them regardless of `line-height` — the styling recipe added 2026-08-27 didn't account for this, so the email would have rendered as a cramped wall of text without her manual fix. **Fixed in the standing recipe** (see `context/hubspot-marketing.md`'s Email Visual Styling Recipe) — future drafts should set explicit `margin-bottom` on every `<p>` rather than relying on spacer paragraphs.
- **Likely accidental, flagged not fixed:** `baseFontStyle.fontFamily` (the document-level base style, not the per-paragraph inline styles) now reads `"'PingFang TC', 微軟正黑體, 'Microsoft JhengHei', Arial, sans-serif"` — a Traditional Chinese font stack with no connection to anything in this workspace. Every individual paragraph still carries its own correct inline `font-family: Barlow, Arial, sans-serif`, so this likely has no visible effect, but it's almost certainly a HubSpot rich-text-editor artifact (e.g., a font-family dropdown auto-suggesting a locale default) rather than a deliberate choice. Not overwritten in case Jackie is still mid-edit — flagged to her directly instead.
