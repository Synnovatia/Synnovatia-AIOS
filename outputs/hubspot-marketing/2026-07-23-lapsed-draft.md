**Subject:** What I'm Watching: hiring & budgets

**Preview text:** The hiring math worth checking every quarter, and why client budgets are harder to move right now.

Hi [First Name],

A few things I'm keeping an eye on right now, for anyone running a service business in the $250K–$4M range (I know that's a wide range, with very different levels of resources depending on where you land in it — hear me out):

**Financing costs are still shaping hiring decisions.** Here's the math worth checking every quarter: add up what a new hire really costs you — salary, taxes, benefits, and the time it takes them to get up to speed. Divide that by how much extra revenue or work they'll bring in each month. That tells you how many months it takes to earn back what you spent.

**Specialized roles are taking longer to fill than they used to.** Before you set a hiring timeline, check it against what's actually happening: how long did it really take to fill your last specialized hire, compared to what you expected? Have you asked your recruiter or your network lately how long it's taking to fill this kind of role right now? Hiring has changed a lot in the last couple of years, so we need a new set of decision-making skills to match it.

**Client budgets are harder to move, even for businesses that are doing fine.** Forrester's 2026 research found that finance teams are getting involved earlier in buying decisions, and leaders are under more pressure to justify every dollar they spend. Bank of America's small business data shows the same thing: owners holding onto cash and slowing down new spending, even when business is good. If a client's approval process feels slower than it used to, that's the economy, not a problem with what you're offering. But it means you need a clear, specific answer ready for "why this, why now."

Jackie
